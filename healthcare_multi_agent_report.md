### Potential warning signs (red‑flags) that you should look out for during your visit

| Red‑flag symptom | Why it matters | What to ask / look for |
|------------------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Sudden, “worst‑ever” headache** | Could indicate a bleed, aneurysm, or other serious event | Ask if the pain was abrupt and how intense it feels (scale 0‑10). |
| **Headache with fever, stiff neck, rash, or confusion** | Suggests meningitis or encephalitis | Check for temperature, neck stiffness, altered mental status. |
| **Vision changes (blur, double vision, loss of vision)** | May signal increased intracranial pressure, stroke, or vascular event | Ask about visual symptoms, check visual acuity if possible. |
| **Sudden weakness, numbness, slurred speech, or loss of balance** | Possible stroke or other neurologic emergency | Inquire about focal neurological deficits. |
| **Severe vomiting or nausea that follows the headache** | Can accompany migraines or increased ICP | Ask about vomiting frequency, relation to pain. |
| **Headache that awakens you from sleep** | Often a sign of more serious pathology | Note if the headache woke you up and if it is worse in the morning. |
| **Headache that worsens with coughing, straining, or exercise** | Suggests increased intracranial pressure | Ask if pain changes with physical activity. |
| **Recent head injury** | Trauma can cause delayed complications | Verify any fall or bump to the head in the past week. |
| **New neurological symptoms** | Could indicate evolving disease | Look for changes in sensation, coordination, or vision. |

> **If any of the above red‑flag symptoms are present, seek immediate medical care.** Call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department right away. Do not wait for a routine appointment.
